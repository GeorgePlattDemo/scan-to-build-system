Define your project to your space — and your taste

A scan gets the shape. You get the numbers.

Start with a scan if you have one. Your phone can capture the room — the walls, the opening, roughly where things sit. That gives us context: what kind of space this is, what's around it, which way the light comes in.

A scan is not a measurement. It's close, and close is fine for context. It isn't fine for a part that has to slide into a 45½-inch opening with a quarter inch to spare.

So you take the ones that matter. Width at the top, the middle, and the bottom — they're rarely the same. Height on both sides. Depth front to back. A tape does this. So does a laser, if you have one. A level tells you what's out of plumb, which matters more than most people expect.

Some numbers are about taste, not fit. Where the top lands is your call. Sixty-five inches, say. Line a shelf up with the mantel and it looks deliberate. Ignore the mantel and it looks like it landed there. Either is fine — it's your room.

You decide which numbers are controlling. Mark a dimension as controlling and that's what the parts get cut to. Everything else is context we keep and don't cut to. Nobody but you has stood in that room.

What happened

One project. One version history. One record.

Written forward as it happened, not assembled backwards from what survived.

SOURCE
YOU PROVIDED — [files · measurements · notes · drawings]

Original sources retained where available. The file stays the file.

OBSERVATIONS
WE RECORDED — [measurements · stated requirements · unresolved conditions]

What was observed stayed separate from what was assumed. Where the two could not be separated, the condition stayed unresolved.

CONFIGURATION
YOU CHOSE — [project choices]

These became part of the identified version. The ones you were not asked about were not choices.

DEFINITION
VERSION — [version id]

The definition was confirmed before any commercial processing began.

YARD REQUEST
SUBMITTED — [date · request id]

The request carried the confirmed version plus your service and handoff choices.

A request was not an order.

YARD RESPONSE
RETURNED — [accepted · adjusted · declined]

Changes were stated explicitly, with reasons.

Confirmed geometry was not silently rewritten.

OFFER
TERMS — [offer id · validity · amount]

One offer, one identified version, one stated period.

ACCEPTANCE
DECISION — [accepted · declined · expired]

Recorded separately from payment. Expired is an outcome, not a gap.

SETTLEMENT
PAYMENT — [status · reference]

Recorded separately from allocation and from release.

MATERIAL
ALLOCATED — [material identity · quantity · lot / source]

What the Store committed to this job, and where it came from.

RELEASE
RELEASE — [status · time]

Release allowed the job to proceed. It did not establish machine readiness.

LOCAL CELL
READINESS — [cell · machine · status]

The cell established its own readiness. Cycle Start remained local.

MACHINE OUTCOME
PRIMARY WORK — [complete · stopped · exception]

Each required operation kept its recorded outcome, including the ones that did not finish.

SECONDARY WORK
COMPLETION — [none · completed · customer · third party]

Residual work resolved according to the accepted completion plan. Where it went to you, that is recorded as an assignment, not an omission.

QUALITY
INSPECTION — [status]  ·  LABELS — [status]  ·  PART COUNT — [status]

Three separate checks. A pass on one is not a pass on the others.

STAGING
STAGED — [time]  ·  READY NOTICE — [time]

Staged did not mean custody transferred.

HANDOFF
CUSTODY — [pickup · delivery · recipient · time]

Custody transferred here.

CLOSED
PROJECT STATUS — closed

Closed means handed over and reconciled. It does not mean nothing is left to do — assembly, finish and fixing were always yours.

The final record preserves the chain:

source → observation → configuration → definition → request →
response → offer → acceptance → settlement → allocation →
release → production → completion → inspection → staging → custody

No redraw.
No silent substitution.
No missing step promoted by wording.

Read backwards, it is an audit. Read forwards, it is only what happened.

The record is not valuable because it is thorough. It is valuable because it is checkable, and because it is yours to take with you — to the next project, to a different yard, or to somebody who wants to know why a recorded dimension is what it is.
