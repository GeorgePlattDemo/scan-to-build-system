01 · STORE ZEROwhat is answering, and what its answers establish

Store Zero

A declared reference lumberyard. Read this before reading anything it says.

Store Zero is a declared reference lumberyard. It is not a real dealer and it is not a mock-up. It is a complete, pinned answering surface, built so that a request has something specific to be answered by. It carries a catalog of SKUs with unit prices and a catalog clock. It carries declared on-hand quantities, which are stated figures and not physical counts. It carries a declared machine envelope for dimensional cell D-001 and sheet cell S-001, a declared list of the secondary operations it will and will not perform, declared economics covering mark-on, cell recovery and the cycle model, and a named owner and cell steward whose resolution authority is declared per condition class.

Every answer it returns carries its basis and its pins: which catalog clock, which engine version, which commit. That is what makes receipt of an order auditable rather than merely plausible. Send the same request twice against the same pins and the same number comes back. Send it after something moved and a different number comes back, and the record will say what moved, when, and who declared it. Store Zero holds no commercial authority, no live inventory, no payment, and no production release.

MENARDS REFERENCE SHEET SHELF — MERCHANT PRICE × 1.05

Reference merchant shelf, not live Store inventory. Menards Everyday Low Price is retained as the merchant basis and Store Zero derives its reference shelf price by applying the declared 5% mark-on. Expired rebate values are not used. Store Zero on-hand remains unresolved until a Store declaration establishes it.

THE NON-CLAIM

The word declared is load-bearing and is used the same way everywhere in this system: declared, never inferred. A declared figure is a real, citable, checkable figure. It is not a measurement. The word pins is load-bearing in the same way: an answer without its catalog clock, engine version and commit is not reproducible, and an answer that cannot be reproduced cannot be audited.

03 · SECONDARY OPERATIONSthe canonical catalog — 28 operations, 4 families, every field

Secondary operations catalog

What the cells do not do, and who does it instead. This is the single definition of these operations; the request screen above selects from it and does not keep its own copy.

An operation is declared or it is not. A node that has not declared a capability does not have it, however obviously it looks like it should. Undeclared operations stay visible here and route outward rather than disappearing — a missing capability is a fact about the network, and worth being able to see. Declared capability is not commissioned capability.
This catalog cannot be totalled. The bases differ — per part, per hole, per linear foot, per column, per pack, per job, quoted, rate call. An operation price exists only once a quantity from an actual part list is applied to it, which is what the request screen does and what this page deliberately does not. A placeholder amount is not a catalog SKU. A rate call is not a price.

04 · WHAT HAPPENS NEXTthe operating chain after acceptance, prospectively

What happens next

Your confirmed version stays unchanged unless you approve a new one.

Nothing below is complicated. It is only kept apart. Each step names who owns it, what it establishes, and what it does not.

01Yard reviewREQUEST · received

The yard checks material, capability, services, timing and current terms against what you asked for.

It may accept, adjust or decline. Every adjustment comes back with a reason.

A reason is not a courtesy. It is what lets you tell a shortage from a preference.

02OfferTERMS · returned

The offer identifies material, services, price, timing and validity. Nothing is reserved.

It is a statement of what the yard would do, for a stated period, against one identified version.

When the period ends, the offer ends. A price quoted against stock that has since gone is not a price.

03AcceptanceYOU · accept or decline

Acceptance binds one version to one offer. Both are identified.

A changed definition requires a new version.

There is no way to amend a version in place. A record you can edit afterwards is not a record.

04SettlementPAYMENT · recorded separately

Acceptance is not payment. Payment is its own event, with its own time and its own reference.

Its status is established before the next commercial step, whatever that step is.

05MaterialSTORE · identifies and allocates

The Store resolves actual material against the accepted job — identity, quantity, lot or source.

Allocation records what is committed to this order, and takes it out of what is available to anyone else.

STORE OPERATIONS, HIGH LEVEL

receive material → identify stock → maintain availability →
allocate to accepted jobs → stage for production → reconcile use,
remnants and exceptions

The Store answers questions about material. It does not answer questions about your project, and it does not change the definition to make an answer easier.

06Production releaseJOB · released

Commercial completion does not start a machine.

Release permits the job to proceed toward the local cell. It confers nothing else.

07Local cellCELL · checks its own readiness

The cell resolves machine, tooling, setup, material presence and local conditions.

Readiness is local knowledge. Nothing upstream can supply it, and nothing upstream can overrule it.

Cycle Start belongs to the person standing at the machine. That is deliberate, and it is not negotiable.

08Primary operationsPARTS · made to the released definition

Each required cycle records its own outcome.

Stopped, incomplete and failed work stays recorded as stopped, incomplete and failed.

A part that was cut twice was cut twice. The second one is the good one; the first one still happened.

09CompletionSECONDARY WORK · resolved

Required secondary work is completed here, assigned elsewhere, or left to you — whichever the accepted plan says. Then: inspect, label, group, stage.

Inspection compares the made part against the confirmed requirement. It does not adjust the requirement to match the part.

10HandoffREADY · notice issued

Standard pickup path: staged within 2 business hours of the last required cycle, then 4 business hours to collect.

The clock starts at the notice, not at staging. You cannot be late for a window nobody told you had opened.

Staged is not picked up.

11CustodyHANDOFF · recorded

Pickup or delivery transfers custody. Until that event the parts are ours to look after and yours to own.

A job is not closed by being finished. It is closed by being handed over.

12Owner recordRECORD · reconciled

One record retains the whole chain:

definition → yard response → accepted terms → settlement →
material → release → machine outcomes → completion →
inspection → staging → custody

Missing events stay missing. Nothing is filled in by assumption, and nothing is promoted by wording.

The chain reads long because it is written down. Every one of these steps happens in an ordinary job too — material gets found, someone decides, a machine runs, someone signs for it. They just happen inside a single word, and the word is usually ordered.

Separating them costs twelve lines of text. Collapsing them costs you the ability to say which one went wrong.

The final record preserves the chain:

source → observation → configuration → definition → request →
response → offer → acceptance → settlement → allocation →
release → production → completion → inspection → staging → custody

No redraw.
No silent substitution.
No missing step promoted by wording.

Read backwards, it is an audit. Read forwards, it is only what happened.

The record is not valuable because it is thorough. It is valuable because it is checkable, and because it is yours to take with you — to the next project, to a different yard, or to somebody who wants to know why a shelf has a 44-inch nominal span.
