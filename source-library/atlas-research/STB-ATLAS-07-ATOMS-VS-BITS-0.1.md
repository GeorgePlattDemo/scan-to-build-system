## 1. Question

Who is already moving information so that finished stuff travels less — and how close is that world to a Scan-to-Build envelope where a job lands at the nearest capable cell instead of a finished picnic table crossing the country?

## 2. Destination role

Parts 1–6 run from idea to iron in one place. This page is the network of places.

Pinned meaning already in force:

- App §20 defers network-wide Store routing.
- Store answers are site-specific: stock, capability, freshness.
- A Store that cannot make a class may defer or refuse. It does not silently ship a national factory’s furniture and call that “local.”
- Production remains closed. This page does not open a marketplace.

## 3. Four distribution models already in the field

They all claim “local” or “distributed.” They move different objects.

| Model | What actually moves | Who matches | Closest STB analog | Wrong steal |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A. Commodity lumber logistics | Boards, bundles, flats | Dealer ERP + big-box “best location” | Stock freshness and pickup | Treating HD delivery as a custom cell |
| B. Yard counter / cut-to-length | A ticket plus a local cut | LBM POS (Part 3) | Site Store + early dimensional capability | App as the POS |
| C. Distributed custom manufacturing | A CAD job to a qualified shop; finished part ships back | Xometry, Fictiv, Protolabs, Hubs | Capability matching by envelope | Metal/print network as a lumber cell |
| D. Microfactory / print farm | Digital twin to a local printer; printed object leaves | Haddy, DIAMOnD-style print grids | Local atoms, wrong material class | Additive furniture as D-001 |

### 3.1 Model A — move the board

Home Depot’s current network is the clearest US example of “ship from best location”: stores, direct fulfillment centers, market delivery ops, and flatbed lumber centers scored by distance, stock, and speed. Relay drops trailers at stores overnight so Pros get boards without emptying the aisle.

That is logistics of **stock**. It is not a machine-neutral job finding a commissioned envelope. Useful later if a Store needs replenishment. Not the Scan-to-Build product.

WorldBids-class lumber bid boards are commodity quotes. Same layer: price the stick, not cut the alcove.

### 3.2 Model B — cut it here

The lumber counter already does the thing people think is futuristic: a length, a SKU, a saw in the same building, carry-out. Dealer POS (Spruce, Flitch, WoodPro) is that ticket.

Scan-to-Build’s first physical Store is closest to this model with a declared bounded capability and governed gates, not a new marketplace.

### 3.3 Model C — route the job, ship the part

Xometry / Fictiv / Protolabs are mature “bits travel, atoms are made somewhere” examples. Matching is real: process, machine size, material, certs, quality history. July–September 2026 Xometry work is explicitly capability-matching shops to jobs.

Limits for Scan-to-Build:

- the object that ships is usually the finished part, often across a region or an ocean
- capability is CNC / sheet / mold / print, not dimensional-lumber cells in a store
- the marketplace becomes the principal; the shop is hidden
- quoting engines invent a price. STB forbids invented price

Steal the *matching idea*: site envelope + stock + freshness. Do not steal the marketplace as the Store.

### 3.4 Model D — print it near the user

Haddy-style LFAM microfactories and peer print marketplaces are a visible “local manufacturing” story in 2026. They do move a file to a nearby printer. The atom is polymer or printed composite, not a 1×6 from the rack. Different envelope, different Store.

Furniture Industry 4.0 plants (Nankang and kin) still concentrate atoms in a factory and ship product. Digital thread inside one plant is not a distributed cell network.

## 4. How close is the field to the STB envelope?

Survey reading, not proof of universal market absence:

- **Local stock:** very close. Yards and big-box already place boards near people.
- **Local square cut:** close. Counters do it daily. No governed class, no WorkPacket, no `POSITION_VALID`.
- **Job routed by declared machine envelope:** close in metal/print marketplaces; not identified in this survey as a lumber-yard cell network.
- **Place-fit opening → local dimensional cell → carry-out part:** this survey did not identify a current product that joins those elements as one offering. That is a candidate market gap, not proof that no one anywhere does it.
- **National custom furniture shipped to the opening:** the opposite of the destination.

The destination is not “become Xometry for wood.” It is: keep Model B, add Parts 1–6 membranes, and only later let a job hop to another Store if this site’s capability or stock fails.

Network-wide routing stays deferred (App §20). Part 7 does not activate it.

## 5. What may be borrowed later

- “best location” as a possible Store-to-Store defer pattern: nearest site whose adopted capability and stock can answer
- marketplace-style capability cards as a field analogy for comparing site envelopes
- yard pickup and flatbed delivery as fulfillment modes the Store already conceptually owns
- print-farm *lesson*: the file moves; the machine stays commissioned and local

## 6. What must not be borrowed

- Xometry as the Store
- instant quote as governed price
- shipping a finished picnic table from a factory and calling it Scan-to-Build
- additive microfactory as D-001
- lumber commodity bidding as project class intake
- opening COLD / CONTRACTOR / multi-Store routing in this atlas
